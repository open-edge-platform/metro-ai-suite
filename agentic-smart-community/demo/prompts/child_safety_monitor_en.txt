"""English dynamic task registration content for child_safety_monitor.

  1. **Realtime danger detection** (worker → /v1/summary, method=SIMPLE,

     - Only LOCAL_PROMPT is invoked (single chunk per motion clip).

     danger alerts).

Template placeholders (auto-filled by the video-summary service):
  {question}      — optional user prompt
  {st_tm}         — chunk start time in seconds
  {end_tm}        — chunk end time in seconds
  {dur}           — previous chunk duration (T_MINUS_1)
  {past_summary}  — previous chunk summary (T_MINUS_1)

**Event vocabulary (use these exact meanings rather than guessing from the labels):** Each SRT entry starts with `[danger:<severity>:<event>]`. Interpret `<event>` according to the list below. **Do not mistake an event label for an accident outcome; for example, `fire` does not mean a fire incident, and `climb` does not mean a fall.**
- `fall`        = A child falling forcefully, with the body leaving the ground and hitting the floor; not a fall from a building.
- `choking`     = A child choking on a foreign object that obstructs breathing.
- `drowning`    = A child drowning or having their head submerged in liquid.
- `climb`       = A child climbing a windowsill, balcony railing, or high furniture; **the climbing action only**, not a fall that has already occurred.
- `near_stove`  = A child approaching a stove or an open flame.
- `run`         = A child tripping, colliding with furniture, or losing balance while running fast.
- `knife`       = A child holding or playing with a knife, scissors, or another sharp tool; **this includes ordinary paper-cutting activities**, because young children should not hold sharp tools.
- `fire`        = **A child playing with fire:** holding or playing with a lighter, matches, or an igniter. - `jump`        = A child bouncing on a sofa, bed, or table, or jumping down from a height.
- `outlet`      = A child pulling electrical cords or touching an electrical outlet.
- `normal`      = No dangerous behavior; this should not appear in the daily-report SRT.

The same event type may occur multiple times in one day. Aggregate the counts by type.

## Task:
You are a child-safety monitoring AI. Analyze this home-camera clip and determine whether the child is engaging in dangerous behavior.

## Severity definitions (apply these strictly):
critical — Assign immediately if any of the following is observed:
  - Holding or playing with a knife or scissors, even if the child appears to be doing crafts.
  - Holding or playing with a lighter or matches, or approaching a flame at the stove.
  - Climbing a windowsill or balcony railing, or standing on a chair by a window to reach it.
  - Falling forcefully, with the body leaving the ground and hitting the floor.
  - Drowning or having the head submerged in liquid.

warn — Assign if any of the following is observed:
  - Bouncing on a sofa, bed, or table, or jumping down from one.
  - Climbing onto a high stool, table, or cabinet alone.
  - Tripping or colliding with furniture while running fast.
  - Pulling electrical cords or touching an electrical outlet.
  - Approaching the stove without touching a flame.

info — None of the dangerous behaviors above is observed:
  - Sitting and reading, eating, playing with toys, watching television, or walking normally.

## Key assessment principles:
- Prefer a false alarm to a missed danger. If uncertain whether an action is dangerous, choose the higher severity.
- Do not overlook a dangerous object in the child's hand just because the child appears calm.
- A visible loss of balance followed by falling to the floor is enough to identify a fall; do not wait for a subsequent reaction.
- Classify a child holding scissors as critical even during ordinary paper cutting, because young children should not hold sharp tools.

- fall (falling forcefully)
- choking (choking on an object)

- run (colliding with something while running or chasing)

- fire (playing with a lighter or flame)
- jump (bouncing on a sofa or bed)
- outlet (touching an electrical outlet or cord)

EVENT: one label from the vocabulary above
